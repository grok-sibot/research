When a person is half-hidden, two distance guesses disagree

A camera already guesses how far away a pedestrian is in two ways: from how tall they look in the frame, and from a monocular depth map’s nearest surface inside their box. Occlusion barely changes the tallness cue, but it pulls the depth cue toward the thing in front. That disagreement is itself an occlusion detector — no training, no occlusion labels, and no need for focal length or stature at ranking time.

Chibueze Praise Onyenweaku, Toyibat Adele, Sikirulahi Opeyemi Abdulkareem & Obed Honour Eje

Why it matters

Self-driving cars and delivery robots often only see part of a person, and knowing “this detection is half-hidden” lets them widen uncertainty or slow down even when they cannot fill in the missing pixels. The authors show that signal can be read for free from two distance numbers a camera pipeline already computes, and that it still works when a detector trained on German roads loses about half its accuracy on street scenes in Lagos — exactly the kind of place where fresh occlusion labels are hard to get.

The paper

Occlusion for Free: Detecting Occlusion from the Disagreement Between Two Monocular Distance Cues

Chibueze Praise Onyenweaku (University of Lagos), Toyibat Adele (ML Collective), Sikirulahi Opeyemi Abdulkareem and Obed Honour Eje (Federal University of Technology, Minna / ML Collective). arXiv:2611.06899 [cs.CV, cs.AI], submitted ~4 Nov 2026. Read on arXiv · PDF

In plain language: a YOLOv8 detector draws a box around each pedestrian. Apparent height inverts the pinhole camera, zapp = fy·S / hpx, and stays roughly stable under occlusion because KITTI-style boxes still span the whole person. Monocular metric depth (Depth Anything V2 outdoor) reads zdep = k·dmin from the nearest surface inside the box — often the occluder — so it is pulled closer. Combining the calibration terms into a gain G gives

zdep / zapp = G · dmin · hpx

Only ranking matters, so G drops out and the deployable score is simply dmin·hpx (lower → more occluded). On KITTI it reaches heavy-occlusion AUC 0.909 (0.888 on all annotated pedestrians with annotated boxes), any-occlusion AUC 0.826, and partly 0.775. Within distance bands the heavy AUC stays 0.894–0.925 while true distance alone sits near chance (0.457–0.594). On CARLA with measured visibility it reaches 0.819 vs 0.644 for distance alone. An example frame: visible pedestrian ~10.5 m vs ~10.7 m; occluded ~20.8 m vs ~13.6 m (~34% disagreement).

  • Not a range proxy. Occluded people are a bit farther on average, but holding distance fixed the score still works and raw distance does not.
  • Needs metric depth with near-pure gain. Ranking matches the calibrated signed ratio (0.909 vs 0.908). Among visible pedestrians, correlation of the score with distance is ρ≈−0.07 for DAv2; a synthetic +2 m depth offset pushes ρ to ≈−0.61 (Eq. 2).
  • Motivated by transfer failure. Depth fusion, occlusion augmentation and attention did not fix out-of-distribution detection in the configurations tested; a KITTI-trained detector dropped from 0.8809 to 0.4482 mAP50 on 350 Lagos frames.

Limits noted in the paper: the score ranks occlusion presence (and coarse partly/heavy categories), not a continuous occlusion fraction; it is validated on daytime pedestrians only; a detector that shrinks occluded boxes would weaken the tallness cue; calibration-free ranking held for the outdoor DAv2 checkpoint on KITTI but not for an indoor checkpoint at robot height on CARLA; dense crowds, night/rain, other depth models and embedded hardware are untested; Lagos annotations have no occlusion labels so the score itself was not scored there.

How to use it

  • Match the paper’s frame: tap Visible, then Occluded. Apparent height and depth should agree near 10.5 m / 10.7 m when clear, and diverge to about 20.8 m / 13.6 m (~34%) when blocked — and the ranking score should drop.
  • Prove calibration cancels: move fy, S or k. Calibrated metres change, but dmin·hpx does not. Ranking only needs the two raw measurements.
  • Simulate an occluder: keep box height high and drag dmin down — the stick figure stays tall in the box while the depth marker jumps to the nearer surface.
  • Check the confound: read the band table and bars: score AUC stays ~0.89–0.93 inside each band; distance alone collapses. Then push the offset slider and watch ρ among visible people worsen from ≈−0.07 toward ≈−0.61 at +2 m — the deployment check from Eq. 2.
