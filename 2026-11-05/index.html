Low-carbon cement that stores CO₂, watched as it sets

Ordinary Portland cement releases CO₂ twice: from burning fuel, and from baking limestone, which gives off CO₂ as it turns into lime. A binder made from magnesium oxide (MgO) and silica avoids the limestone step, and adding a magnesium carbonate locks CO₂ into the hardened material. Princeton researchers used a synchrotron X-ray beam to watch these pastes react every 20 minutes for their first day, and found the strength-giving gel starts forming within an hour, earlier than the textbook sequence says.

Jordan Mravca-Bailey & Claire E. White

Why it matters

Cement production causes 7–8% of global human CO₂ emissions, and most of that comes from baking limestone, so a binder that skips limestone and permanently stores about 12–15% CO₂ by weight in its powder attacks the problem from both ends. Knowing that the strength-giving magnesium silicate hydrate gel forms within the first hour, and that a carbonate additive gets all the MgO reacting within a day instead of more than a week, gives engineers the timing they need to design mixes that set reliably on a building site.

The paper

Early-age formation of MgO-SiO₂ binders and the effect of hydromagnesite and dehydrated hydromagnesite: An in situ synchrotron X-ray diffraction and pair distribution function study

Jordan Mravca-Bailey and Claire E. White (Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering and Andlinger Center for Energy and the Environment, Princeton University). arXiv:2611.03929 [cond-mat.mtrl-sci], submitted 2 Nov 2026. Read on arXiv · PDF

In plain language: the team mixed three pastes of reactive MgO, silica fume (SF, a fine glassy silica) and water. One had nothing else, one had hydromagnesite (HY, a hydrated magnesium carbonate, Mg₅(CO₃)₄(OH)₂·4H₂O), and one had the same mineral after heating it to 325 °C to drive out its water (DHY). They sealed each paste in a 1 mm tube and recorded X-ray diffraction, which sees crystals, and pair-distribution-function (PDF) data, which also sees disordered gels, roughly every 20 minutes for 24 hours, then again at 1, 3, 7, 14 and 42 days.

  • The gel comes first. Magnesium silicate hydrate (M-S-H), the main strength-giving phase, was already present in the first scan at about 45 minutes in all three pastes, before any brucite (Mg(OH)₂) or its carbonate-bearing cousin (HCB) had appeared. So early M-S-H forms directly from dissolving MgO and silica, not only via brucite.
  • Carbonate speeds up the MgO. With HY or DHY, all of the MgO had dissolved by 24 hours; without it, about 12% was left and some MgO survived past 7 days.
  • Dehydrated carbonate delays things, then wins. DHY soaked water back in during the first ~10 hours, which slowed MgO dissolution and held back HCB until ~6.5 h, but by 42 days the DHY paste had the most M-S-H: 1.5 times the HY paste and 1.8 times the plain paste.

Limits noted in the paper: the two carbonate pastes do not have equal CO₂ content, so the effect of disorder in DHY cannot yet be separated from dosage; the MgO dissolution extent is measured from the first scan at about 1 hour, so earlier dissolution is missed; brucite/HCB peak areas are in arbitrary units and are compared only by their timing. Separately, the samples were 1 mm capillaries and this study did not test strength.

How to use it

  • Check the paper’s numbers: tap the three presets. The calculator should show Mg/Si of 0.99 (the paper rounds it to 1.00), 1.54 and 1.63 and CO₂ of 0, 12.4% and 14.7%, matching Table 1.
  • Trade CO₂ for magnesium balance: push the carbonate slider up and watch stored CO₂ climb, but the Mg/Si ratio rises too, because the carbonate also brings magnesium. The paper notes that above Mg/Si ≈ 1.2 leftover brucite can persist for years.
  • Scrub through the first day: drag the time slider. Around 2 h the plain and HY pastes start growing brucite or HCB; the DHY paste waits until about 6.5 h while about a third of its MgO has already dissolved, and that magnesium goes into M-S-H gel.
  • Compare all three: the plain paste (blue) never quite finishes dissolving its MgO in a day; both carbonate pastes do.
